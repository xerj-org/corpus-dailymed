DailyMed e76f35a5-711c-c511-9fd8-b8c4c0b43b85 | Deflazacort Oral Suspension | 5.7 Ophthalmic Effects

Use of corticosteroids, including deflazacort, may produce posterior subcapsular cataracts. Corticosteroids may also cause glaucoma with possible damage to the optic nerves, and may increase the risk of secondary ocular infections caused by bacteria, fungi, or viruses.

                        Corticosteroids are not recommended for patients with active ocular herpes simplex.

                        Intraocular pressure may become elevated in some patients taking corticosteroids. If treatment with deflazacort is continued for more than 6 weeks, monitor intraocular pressure.
