Acromegaly

                        The recommended starting dosage of lanreotide injection is 90 mg given via the deep subcutaneous route, at 4-week intervals for 3 months.

                        After 3 months, the dosage may be adjusted as follows:

                           GH greater than 1 ng/mL to less than or equal to 2.5 ng/mL, IGF-1 normal, and clinical symptoms controlled: maintain lanreotide injection dosage at 90 mg every 4 weeks.

                           GH greater than 2.5 ng/mL, IGF-1 elevated, and/or clinical symptoms uncontrolled: increase lanreotide injection dosage to 120 mg every 4 weeks.

                           GH less than or equal to 1 ng/mL, IGF-1 normal, and clinical symptoms controlled: reduce lanreotide injection dosage to 60 mg every 4 weeks.

                        Thereafter, the dosage should be adjusted according to the response of the patient as judged by a reduction in serum GH and/or IGF-1 levels; and/or changes in symptoms of acromegaly.

                        Patients who are controlled on lanreotide injection 60 mg or 90 mg may be considered for an extended dosing interval of lanreotide injection 120 mg every 6 or 8 weeks. GH and IGF-1 levels should be obtained 6 weeks after this change in dosing regimen to evaluate persistence of patient response.

                        Continued monitoring of patient response with dosage adjustments for biochemical and clinical symptom control, as necessary, is recommended.

                           Gastroenteropancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumors (GEP-NETs)

                        The recommended dosage of lanreotide injection is 120 mg administered every 4 weeks by deep subcutaneous injection.

                           Carcinoid Syndrome

                        The recommended dosage of lanreotide injection is 120 mg administered every 4 weeks by deep subcutaneous injection.

                        If patients are already being treated with lanreotide injection for GEP-NETs, do not administer an additional dose for the treatment of carcinoid syndrome.
