DailyMed fc726765-5d5a-4d6e-b037-b847bda9fb7c | Kerendia | 14.2 CKD associated with T1DM

Use of Kerendia for this indication is supported by evidence from adequate and well-controlled studies in adults with CKD associated with T2DM (FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD) with additional pharmacodynamic and safety data in adults with CKD associated with T1DM. Kerendia improved renal outcomes and reduced UACR in adults with CKD associated with T2DM in the FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD studies. Therefore, the effect on UACR was used to infer a reduction in the risk of sustained eGFR decline and end-stage kidney disease in adults with CKD associated with T1DM.

                        FINE-ONE (NCT: 05901831) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter study in 242 adults with CKD associated with T1DM. Patients were required to have an UACR ≥200 mg/g to <5000 mg/g at screening, HbA1c <10%, eGFR 25 to <90 mL/min/1.73m2, and serum potassium ≤4.8 mEq/L, be continuously treated with insulin, and be receiving a stable dose of an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACEi) or angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) as standard of care. Patients treated with sodium glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists were excluded from the study. Patients were randomized to receive either Kerendia (N=120) or placebo (N=122) over 6 months. The starting and target dose of Kerendia was the same as the FIDELIO-DKD and FIGARO-DKD studies.

                        The primary endpoint in FINE-ONE was the change in log-transformed UACR from baseline (evaluated as the ratio to baseline) averaged over Months 3 and 6. Treatment with Kerendia reduced UACR compared to placebo over 6 months (p=0.0001). At Months 3 and 6, the adjusted relative reduction in UACR comparing Kerendia with placebo was 22% (ratio of Least Square Geometric Mean Ratio (LSGMR) of 0.78; 95% CI: 0.68, 0.90) and 28% (ratio of LSGMR of 0.72; 95% CI: 0.60, 0.86), respectively.
