Overdosage of insulin may cause hypoglycemia with accompanying symptoms that include listlessness, confusion, palpitations, sweating, vomiting, and headache.

                  Hypoglycemia and hypokalemia may occur as a result of an excess of ONSWIK relative to food intake, energy expenditure, or both [see Warnings and Precautions (5.3, 5.6)]. Mild episodes of hypoglycemia usually can be treated with oral carbohydrates. Lowering the dosage of ONSWIK, adjustments in meal patterns, or physical activity may be needed.

                  More severe episodes with coma, seizure, or neurologic impairment may be treated with glucagon or concentrated intravenous glucose. Sustained carbohydrate intake and observation may be necessary, because hypoglycemia may recur after apparent clinical recovery. Hypokalemia must be corrected appropriately.

                  Consideration of the prolonged duration of action of ONSWIK is recommended in the management of overdose [see Dosage and Administration (2.6), Warnings and Precautions (5.3), Clinical Pharmacology (12.2)].
