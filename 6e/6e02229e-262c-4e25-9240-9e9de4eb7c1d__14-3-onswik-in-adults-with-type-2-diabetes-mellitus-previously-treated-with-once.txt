DailyMed 6e02229e-262c-4e25-9240-9e9de4eb7c1d | ONSWIK KwikPen | 14.3 ONSWIK in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Previously Treated with Once- or Twice-Daily Basal Insulin

Trial 3 (QWINT-3, NCT05275400) was a 78-week, randomized, open-label, active-controlled, treat-to-target, multinational trial that evaluated the efficacy of once-weekly subcutaneous ONSWIK in 986 adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus on once- or twice-daily subcutaneous basal insulin therapy alone or in combination with noninsulin antidiabetic medication(s) with a HbA1c of 6.5% to 10%. Patients were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to either once-weekly ONSWIK (N=655) or once-daily insulin degludec (N=331) administered according to the approved labeling. Patients remained on stable doses of up to three of the following noninsulin antidiabetic medications: DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, biguanides such as metformin, alpha-glucosidase inhibitors, or GLP-1 receptor agonists. The primary endpoint was assessed at Week 26.

                        The mean age was 61 years, and 56% were male. The mean duration of type 2 diabetes mellitus was 15 years, and the mean BMI was 30 kg/m2. Overall, 67% were White, 28% were Asian, 5% were Black or African American; 0.2% were American Indian or Alaskan Native, 0.1% were Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander, and 0.3% reported multiple races; 27% identified as Hispanic or Latino ethnicity.

                        ONSWIK was initiated at a one-time starting dosage calculated by multiplying the prior total daily basal insulin dosage by 7 and then multiplying by 3. For patients with a baseline FBG ≤ 120 mg/dL, a one-time starting dosage was not recommended; the initial ONSWIK dosage was the prior total basal insulin dose multiplied by 7. The second weekly dosage was the prior total daily basal insulin dose multiplied by 7 then the dosage was subsequently adjusted on a weekly basis by 20 to 40 units to achieve a target FBG range of 80 to 120 mg/dL and in response to hypoglycemia. If there was a documented blood glucose ≤70 mg/dL in the previous week, the dose was not increased.

                        Insulin degludec was initiated at the prior total daily basal insulin dosage then was subsequently adjusted on a weekly basis by 2 to 6 units to achieve a target FBG range of 80 to 120 mg/dL and in response to hypoglycemia.

                        At week 26, treatment with ONSWIK provided a mean reduction in HbA1c that met the pre-specified non-inferiority margin (0.4%) (see

                           Table 8).

                        Percent time in glucose range (%TIR,70-180 mg/dL) at baseline was 52.1% for the ONSWIK group and 52.8% for the insulin degludec group. The estimated mean change from baseline in %TIR during the last 4 weeks of the trial (Week 22 to 26) was 8.9% for ONSWIK and 8.4% for insulin degludec.
