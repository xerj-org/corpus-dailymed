DailyMed 6e02229e-262c-4e25-9240-9e9de4eb7c1d | ONSWIK KwikPen | 14.4 ONSWIK in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Previously Treated with Once- or Twice-Daily Basal Insulin and Mealtime Insulin

Trial 4 (QWINT-4, NCT05462756) was a 26-week, randomized, open-label, active-controlled, treat-to-target, multinational trial that evaluated the efficacy of once-weekly subcutaneous ONSWIK in 730 adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus on once- or twice-daily basal insulin and mealtime insulin with a HbA1c of 7% to 10%. Patients were randomized to either once-weekly ONSWIK (N=365) or once-daily insulin glargine (N=365) administered according to the approved labeling. Patients remained on stable doses of up to three of the following noninsulin antidiabetic medications: DPP-4 inhibitors, SGLT2 inhibitors, biguanides such as metformin, or GLP-1 receptor agonists. During the randomized treatment period, patients also received mealtime insulin lispro.

                        The mean age was 59 years, and 50% were male. The mean duration of type 2 diabetes mellitus was 17 years, and the mean BMI was 32 kg/m2. Overall, 69% were White, 14% were Asian, 13% were American Indian or Alaskan Native, and 4% were Black or African American; 55% identified as Hispanic or Latino ethnicity.

                        ONSWIK was initiated at a one-time starting dose calculated by multiplying the prior total daily basal insulin dosage by 7 and then multiplying by 3. For patients with a baseline FBG ≤120 mg/dL, a one-time starting dosage was not recommended; the initial ONSWIK dosage was the prior total daily basal insulin dose multiplied by 7. The second weekly dosage was the prior total daily basal insulin dose multiplied by 7 and was subsequently adjusted on a weekly basis by 20 to 40 units to achieve a target FBG range of 80 to 120 mg/dL and in response to hypoglycemia. If there was a documented blood glucose ≤70 mg/dL in the previous week, the dose was not increased.

                        Insulin glargine was initiated at the prior total daily basal insulin dosage and was subsequently adjusted on a weekly basis by 3 to 6 units to achieve a target FBG range of 80 to 120 mg/dL and in response to hypoglycemia.

                        At week 26, treatment with ONSWIK provided a mean reduction in HbA1c that met the pre-specified non-inferiority margin (0.4%) (see

                           Table 9).

                        Percent time in glucose range (%TIR,70-180 mg/dL) at baseline was 44.0% for the ONSWIK group and 43.3% for the insulin glargine group. The estimated mean change from baseline in %TIR during the last 4 weeks of the trial (Week 22 to 26) was 14.4% for ONSWIK and 13.1% for insulin glargine.
