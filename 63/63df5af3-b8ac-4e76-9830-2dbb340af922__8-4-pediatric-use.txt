Children and Adolescent Participants with HIV 1

                        The safety, pharmacokinetics, and effectiveness of TIVICAY and TIVICAY PD were evaluated in 75 treatment-naïve or treatment-experienced, INSTI-naïve pediatric and adolescent participants with HIV‑1 aged 4 weeks to less than 18 years weighing at least 3 kg in an open-label, multicenter, dose-finding clinical trial, IMPAACT P1093 [see Adverse Reactions (6.1), Clinical Pharmacology (12.3), Clinical Studies (14.3)]. Additional pharmacokinetics data were evaluated in 2 pharmacokinetic substudies in ODYSSEY, an open-label, randomized, non-inferiority trial to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetic parameters of TIVICAY or TIVICAY PD plus two NRTIs compared with standard of care in pediatric participants with HIV‑1 younger than 18 years [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)].

                        Overall, the safety data in pediatric participants from the IMPAACT P1093 trial were comparable to those observed in adults [see Adverse Reactions (6.1)]. The pharmacokinetic parameters of TIVICAY or TIVICAY PD in pediatric participants from IMPAACT P1093 and ODYSSEY were comparable to those of adults receiving 50 mg once daily or twice daily [see Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)]. The effectiveness observed in IMPAACT P1093 is comparable to that of treatment-experienced adult participants.

                           Neonates Exposed to HIV‑1

                        The safety and pharmacokinetics of dolutegravir were evaluated in 48 term HIV‑1 exposed neonates in a Phase I, open-label, multicenter clinical study (IMPAACT 2023). Dolutegravir was orally administered in 2 cohorts of neonates exposed to HIV‑1, weighing at least 2 kg, receiving standard of care antiretroviral prophylaxis in the first 4 to 6 weeks of life. All enrolled neonates were followed for safety data for a duration of 16 weeks. Neonates in cohorts 1 and 2 were grouped based on dolutegravir exposure in utero. Cohort 1 included 18 neonates (12 were exposed, and 6 were unexposed to dolutegravir in utero) and received 2 single doses of dolutegravir: the first dose was administered within 5 days of birth, and the second dose 7 days later. Cohort 2 included 30 neonates (16 were exposed, and 14 were unexposed to dolutegravir in utero) who received a chronic dolutegravir dosing regimen through 4 to 6 weeks of life. Demographics included 63% female; 54% Black, 33% Asian, 6% White, 4% unknown, and 2% multi-racial. Among the 43 neonates with HIV test results available at Week 16 (End of Study), all were HIV-1 negative. Estimated dolutegravir exposures in neonates were higher compared to older pediatric and adult populations receiving dolutegravir once daily; however, the safety profile was similar [see Adverse Reactions (6.1), Clinical Pharmacology (12.3)].

                        Safety and effectiveness of TIVICAY or TIVICAY PD have not been established in pre‑term neonates, pediatric patients weighing less than 2 kg, or in any pediatric patients who are INSTI‑experienced with documented or clinically suspected resistance to other INSTIs (e.g., raltegravir, elvitegravir).
